VEIL — 3-MINUTE PITCH SCRIPT (DRAFT v2 — narrative structure)
Audience: senior/higher management at an organisation running air-gapped environments across
different sensitivity classes (e.g. a high-side and a lower-side network with no connectivity
between them).

STRUCTURE: hook (vulnerability) -> problem (human fallibility) -> reveal (VEIL, key
differentiator) -> architecture/security -> brief demo (can be mocked) -> summary -> credits.
This replaces the flat Q&A ordering from v1. Every one of the 7 required talking points is still
answered — see the [Q#] tag on each section for where.

IMPORTANT — keep this framing honest throughout: VEIL is decision support for the person doing
the review. It does not itself authorise a release or change a document's classification, and it
does not replace your organisation's existing approval process. That line is built into the REVEAL
section below as "it doesn't decide for you" — don't let editing soften it into implying VEIL signs
off on anything.

============================================================
0. HOOK
============================================================
[~15 sec / ~35 words]

One email. One report, sent to the wrong side of the boundary. One detail someone missed. That's
all it takes for a career-ending data leak — and that risk is sitting inside every document we
send out of a sensitive environment, every single day.

SHOW: Cold open — dark title card or a blurred document, no app UI yet.
ON-SCREEN TEXT: "One missed detail. One career-ending leak."

============================================================
1. THE PROBLEM — covers [Q2: why] + [Q3: what problem]
============================================================
[~30 sec / ~70 words]

Desensitising a document by hand is slow enough on its own — someone has to read every line and
catch anything sensitive before it moves. But the real danger isn't the effort. It's what a human
reviewer misses: an unlabeled name, a detail with no obvious pattern. Or worse — a name kept in
one place and a figure kept in another, each looking harmless alone, but together pointing
straight at one person, one deal. Once that crosses the boundary, you can't pull it back.

SHOW: A document with an obviously-labeled name masked, but a bare surname and a dollar figure
still sitting in plain text elsewhere in the same document.
ON-SCREEN TEXT: "Alone: harmless. Together: identifiable. And you can't un-send it."

============================================================
2. THE REVEAL — VEIL — covers [Q1: what you built] + [Q5: what makes it different]
============================================================
[~35 sec / ~85 words]

This is why we built VEIL.

VEIL finds the obvious sensitive details automatically — names, emails, IDs, financial figures —
and it catches what a human reviewer typically misses: unlabeled names, and combinations of
"safe" details that together aren't safe at all.

But here's the part that matters most: VEIL doesn't make the call for you. It surfaces every
risk, explains why it's flagged, and puts the decision in your hands. You decide what gets
desensitised. VEIL's job is making sure you never miss what you needed to decide on.

SHOW: VEIL's logo/name card, then cut into the app — findings list populating on the example memo.
ON-SCREEN TEXT: "VEIL doesn't decide for you. It makes sure YOU don't miss anything."
(This is the single most important line in the video — protect it in editing.)

============================================================
3. ARCHITECTURE & SECURITY — covers [Q4: how it addresses it] + part of [Q7: technically cool]
============================================================
[~30 sec / ~70 words]

Under the hood: pattern detection for the obvious cases, an optional AI pass for the ones that
aren't, and a relationship engine that flags dangerous combinations — every suggestion
independently checked against the source document before it's ever shown to you.

And critically: everything runs locally, in the browser. No cloud calls, no external network,
ever, by default — which is the only way a tool like this could sit inside an air-gapped,
classified environment at all, reviewing documents without anything leaving the boundary it's
protecting.

SHOW: A simple architecture beat — three boxes (Pattern detection / Local AI / Relationship
engine) all inside a single "your machine, no network" boundary — then the AI-assisted panel with
the "sent to your AI endpoint this session" badge, pointed at localhost.
ON-SCREEN TEXT: "No cloud. No external calls. Built to run inside the boundary."

============================================================
4. BRIEF DEMO (can be a mock walkthrough) — covers [Q6: key features]

[~35 sec / ~65 words]

Here's VEIL on a fictional vendor memo. Findings appear instantly. An unlabeled name gets caught.
The relationship graph flags a name and a figure that are too closely tied to keep separately.
You decide — mask or keep, item by item — and only once everything's been reviewed does VEIL let

SHOW: Fast-cut mock walkthrough — load doc, findings populate, click a flagged relationship in the

ON-SCREEN TEXT (one per cut): "Findings, instantly" / "Catches what you'd miss" / "You decide" /

============================================================
5. SUMMARY — covers remainder of [Q7: technically cool] as a closing beat
============================================================
[~20 sec / ~45 words]

VEIL is the review step between a sensitive document and the outside world. It doesn't replace
your judgement — it makes sure your judgement has everything it needs, verified, and never sent
anywhere it shouldn't be. The best redaction tool isn't the one that decides for you. It's the one
that makes sure you never miss what you needed to decide on.

SHOW: Return to the VEIL name card.
ON-SCREEN TEXT: "VEIL — the review step you can't afford to skip."
